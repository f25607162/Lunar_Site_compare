Why the south pole is so hard

Near the poles the Moon’s axis is almost upright, so the Sun circles the horizon and never climbs more than about 1.5° above it. A ridge a few kilometres away can mean near-constant light, while a crater floor stays dark for years.

Earth hangs in the sky only for people on the near side. From the south pole it sits close to the horizon and swings with the Moon’s libration, so communication windows open and close.

Without air there is no twilight or refraction: the Sun is either above the terrain or it is not.

Positions come from low-precision lunar and solar series calculated in your browser (about 0.3° error). Terrain is a flat horizon angle you set; real LOLA elevation profiles are not loaded. Site coordinates are approximate. Check final numbers against JPL Horizons.
